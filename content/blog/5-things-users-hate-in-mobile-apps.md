People decide very quickly whether an app is worth keeping. Most apps don't lose users because of one big failure. They lose them because of small frustrations that pile up.

Here are five of the most common ones, and how to fix them.

## 1. Slow loading

If a screen takes too long to load, people assume the app is broken.

**Fix it:** show something useful straight away, such as a skeleton screen or cached data, while fresh data loads in the background. Compress images and only load what the screen actually needs.

## 2. Confusing onboarding

Long sign-up forms and tutorials before people can try anything make many users give up.

**Fix it:** let people see value first. Ask only for what you need right now, and collect the rest later.

## 3. Asking for every permission at once

Requesting location, contacts, camera and notifications the moment the app opens feels invasive.

**Fix it:** ask for each permission at the moment it's needed, and explain why in one simple sentence.

## 4. Too many notifications

Notifications that aren't useful quickly get the app muted or deleted.

**Fix it:** only notify people about things they care about, and let them choose what they receive.

## 5. Crashes and bugs

Nothing breaks trust faster, especially in apps that handle money.

**Fix it:** test on real devices (including older, cheaper phones), monitor crashes after launch, and fix the most common ones first.
