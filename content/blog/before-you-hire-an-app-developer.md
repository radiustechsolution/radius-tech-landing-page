You don't need technical knowledge to start an app project. But a little preparation makes the process faster, clearer and usually cheaper.

Here's what to think about before your first conversation with a developer.

## 1. The problem you're solving

Describe it in one or two sentences. Who has the problem, and how do they deal with it today?

## 2. Who your users are

Customers, staff, vendors, or all three? Different users often need different screens and permissions.

## 3. Must-haves vs nice-to-haves

List every feature you can think of, then split them in two:

- **Must-have:** the app is useless without it
- **Nice-to-have:** it can come in a later version

Launching with only the must-haves gets you to market faster and lets real users tell you what to build next.

## 4. Apps you like

Share two or three apps whose look or experience you admire, and say what you like about each one. This is often clearer than a long written description.

## 5. A rough budget and timeline

You don't need exact numbers. A range helps a developer recommend the right approach for your situation.

## 6. Life after launch

Who will manage content, respond to users and handle updates? Planning for maintenance early avoids surprises later.
